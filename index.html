How the Dojo works

Design constraints for this run: about 30 participants, almost all with a smartphone only (no laptop), and only free AI tools.

The Dojo turns the two-day Enterprise Development & Digital Management block into five levels, from Level 1 Starter to Level 5 Mentor. Each participant takes a five-minute "AI Match Quiz" on her phone (framed like a Facebook personality quiz, not a test, so nobody feels examined), gets a starting level, and follows a path of 6 to 8 practice steps chosen for her business type, skill level, main goal and language. Everyone works on the same six marketing outputs from the slide deck, so the room stays together while each person starts at a different step.

The five levels

LevelShe can…Steps (code for facilitators)Deck link

Safety (S1) is on every path, right after the first step, whatever the level. Participants who rate Q9 (judge AI answers) at 1–2 get it marked as a priority.

How the quiz sets the level

  • Q6–Q10 are plain-language versions of the five CSPS capability items in survey Section E (codes E-C1 to E-C5), on the same 1–5 scale. The Dojo result can be compared with pre/post survey scores.
  • Score (0–100) = capability 50% + AI-use frequency (Q5, same options as survey Q27) 30% + phone skills (Q3) 20%.
  • Q4 (Gmail) does not change the score. Without Gmail, the path flags the Gemini Notebook step as "Needs Gmail" and suggests ChatGPT first; facilitators help make accounts at the break.
  • The quiz has three parts with # headings (#My business, #My phone and AI, #My goals), the same structure participants later use in prompts.
  • Each level shows a one-line "AI forecast", a playful promise of the next result she can reach, which keeps the fortune-telling feel while pointing at a real task.
  • Level cut-offs: Level 1 under 20, Level 2 20–39, Level 3 40–59, Level 4 60–79, Level 5 80+.
  • Step codes (W, Y, G, B, K, S) are for facilitators only and are hidden on the participant screen.
  • Someone who has never used AI starts at Level 1 or 2, however high her self-rating. Her first task is to ask AI one real question.
  • All practice prompts use the same # headings as the Day 1 morning slides (#role, #context, #task, #style, plus #rules where needed), so beginners see one prompt structure all workshop. Y1 teaches the recipe explicitly.
  • Q12 (goal) moves her goal modules to the front of the path. Goal modules below her level stay on the path as a quick round, so every path keeps the six deck tasks in view. Q11 (worry) adds one coaching tip, mirroring the four CSPS barrier items plus scams and mobile data.

Smartphone only, free tools only

  • Tools: ChatGPT free (works without an account, more with one), Gemini free (needs a Google/Gmail account; handles notebook photos), and Meta AI inside Messenger or Facebook where it appears. No paid plan, Canva Pro or laptop is needed for any step.
  • Before 13:00 on 27 Oct: at the lunch break, ask each participant to open chatgpt.com or the Gemini app once and send "Hello". Note who has no Gmail or no data load; pair them with a buddy rather than spending session time on account setup.
  • Phone workflow: Dojo page in one tab, AI app in the other. Copy prompt, switch app, long-press, Paste, change the yellow words, send. Demonstrate this once on the projector from a phone (screen mirroring or a phone camera on the projector), not from a laptop.
  • Typing is slow on a phone: show the microphone button. Prompts are kept short and pre-filled so participants mostly edit the yellow words.
  • Daily limits: free plans cap messages and photo uploads. If one app stops, switch to the other. B1 (notebook photo) is the step most likely to hit a limit, so run it early on Day 2.
  • Privacy: free apps may use chats for training. S1 teaches: no customer names, numbers, MPIN or OTP in any prompt.
  • Seating for about 30: 6 tables of 5, each with at least one Level 4 or 5 as Dojo buddy. If there are fewer than 6, use Level 3 participants with a GCash-paying phone.

On-demand lecture videos

  • What: Dr Murata's recorded "Lead your day" lecture (about 20 min) is split into 5 short videos, one at the top of each part of the Lead your day tab, with English subtitles (on by default). Total about 14 MB (the original file is 35 MB), compressed for mobile data.
  • For whom: women who could not attend the workshop, and participants who want to review. Share the Dojo link with them; they can watch, then do the same activities on their phone.
  • In the room: do not play the videos over venue Wi-Fi for 30 phones at once; the live session covers the same content.

Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM)

  • Why: a notebook makes the AI answer from sources the participant chose, which cuts invented prices and dates, the most common mistake in seller messages. It is free at notebook.google.com (the old notebooklm.google.com link redirects there); sign in with a Google account.
  • For participants (B3, Level 4 and up; goal "save time"): one notebook per business with price list photo, product list, delivery areas and GCash rules. On Day 3 the BCRP draft goes into the same notebook, so the plan stays on her phone and AI can answer "what do I do the day before a typhoon?" from her own plan.
  • For facilitators: a shared "WISE Sensei" notebook with the programme handouts, the BCRP template, the Kabankalan context brief and official PAGASA/PHIVOLCS guidance. Participants ask it questions after the workshop. Audio Overview gives a listen-only summary for those who prefer audio.
  • Check on a phone in Kabankalan before relying on it: that notebook.google.com works in a phone browser on a free Philippine account, and whether a shared notebook opens on mobile (in May 2026 sharing was web-only). If either fails, B3 falls back to pasting the price list into a normal Gemini chat.
  • Privacy: notebooks live in the participant's Google account. Business facts only, no customer names, numbers or GCash details.

Low-connectivity fallback

  • If Wi-Fi fails, run the quiz on paper: the 13 questions fit on one A4 sheet, and facilitators count the score by hand with the rule above.
  • Every practice prompt also works as a group exercise: one phone per table with data, shared by the 5 people.
  • The Dojo page is small. Opening it once on Wi-Fi is enough; the result stays on the phone afterwards.

Collecting answers for analysis

  • How: the page itself stores nothing centrally. "Send my answers" opens a Google Form pre-filled with that participant's quiz answers, level, 24-hour clocks, helper roles, 3 Rs and finished steps. She reads, edits, gives consent and submits. Responses land in a new sheet of the WISE Pre/Post responses spreadsheet.
  • Linking: the optional personal code is the same one as the pre/post surveys (e.g. MA0745), so Dojo data can be joined to Section E scores.
  • Privacy: no names, phones or emails; inputs ask for roles and tasks only. Research use only after ethics approval; until then, programme evaluation only. Consent is required on the form.
  • What we can analyze: level distribution; Q6–Q10 against pre-survey Section E; business type × goal × worry; normal vs disaster-day hours for business, care and community (paired comparison, the care-burden shift in numbers); helper roles and 3 Rs commitments coded into themes; which Dojo steps were finished.
  • Setup (once): run questionnaire/WISE_Dojo_Form.gs in Apps Script and send the log to Claude to connect the form.

Before using this with participants

  • All Hiligaynon lines are drafts. Please have a local co-facilitator check them.
  • Answers are stored in the participant's own browser. They reach the WISE team only if she taps "Send my answers" and submits the form.
  • This is version 1 (26 Oct 2026). Cut-offs are a starting guess and should be tuned after the first run.
